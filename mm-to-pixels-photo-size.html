Converting photo sizes: millimetres, pixels and DPI explained

Some forms describe photo size in centimetres or millimetres, others in pixels. They are connected by one number: dots per inch (DPI).

The formula

pixels = millimetres ÷ 25.4 × DPI  (because one inch is 25.4 mm).

Example: 35 mm at 300 DPI → 35 ÷ 25.4 × 300 ≈ 413 pixels. A 35 × 45 mm photo at 300 DPI is therefore about 413 × 531 pixels.

What DPI really means for an online form

A digital photo has pixels but no physical size until it is printed. If a form asks only for pixels (for example 200 × 230), DPI does not matter — set those pixels exactly. If it asks for millimetres, it is thinking about print, and 300 DPI is the usual print standard.

Keeping the face the right size

Changing the shape of a photo (for example from 4:3 to 35:45) means cropping or adding borders. Crop to fill keeps the photo filling the frame by trimming the edges — position yourself in the centre with space around the head. Fit inside keeps the whole photo and adds white borders.
