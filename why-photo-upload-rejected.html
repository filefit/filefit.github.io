Why your photo or signature upload gets rejected (and how to fix each cause)

You pick a photo, press upload, and the form says “invalid file”. Almost every rejection comes down to one of the nine causes below. Work through them in order and you will usually find the problem in a minute.

1. The file is too large (above the KB limit)

This is the most common cause. A photo straight from a phone camera is usually 2–5 MB, while forms often ask for something like 20–50 KB — about a hundred times smaller. Shrinking a photo means two things: using fewer pixels and saving with stronger JPEG compression. FileFit's Compress to size tries the highest quality that still fits, so the result looks as good as the limit allows.

2. The file is too small (below the minimum)

Many forms also set a minimum, for example “not less than 20 KB”. A small, simple image — especially a signature on white paper — can come out at 4–8 KB even at the best quality. Increasing the quality or the pixel size raises the file size. If the picture is already at full quality, FileFit adds harmless padding inside the JPEG file so it reaches the minimum without changing a single pixel.

3. Wrong pixel dimensions

Some forms check width × height in pixels (for example 200 × 230). A file can be the right number of KB and still be rejected because it is 4000 × 3000 pixels. Set the exact width and height in Photo & signature. Choose Crop to fill to keep the face large, or Fit inside if nothing may be cut off (white borders are added instead).

4. “KB” means a different number to the portal

One kilobyte is 1000 bytes in some systems and 1024 bytes in others. A file of 50,500 bytes is “49.3 KB” on one computer and “50.5 KB” on another — so it can pass on your laptop and fail on the website. The safe approach is to stay below the limit in both meanings for the maximum, and above it in both meanings for the minimum. FileFit does this automatically; this article explains the arithmetic.

5. Wrong file format

Forms usually want JPG/JPEG for photos and signatures and PDF for documents. Renaming photo.png to photo.jpg does not convert it — the file inside is still PNG and strict portals detect that. Re-save the image as a real JPEG; FileFit always outputs genuine JPEG files for photos.

6. The photo is sideways

Phones store “rotate this image” as a separate tag instead of turning the pixels. Some websites ignore the tag and show your photo lying on its side. FileFit applies the rotation to the pixels before saving, so the photo is upright everywhere.

7. Background, face size or lighting

Many applications ask for a plain light background, the face clearly visible and centred, and no heavy shadows. These are checked by people (or software) after upload, so a file can upload fine and still be rejected later. Take the photo facing a window in daylight, stand a little away from the wall to avoid shadows, and fill most of the frame with head and shoulders.

8. Signature too faint or with a grey background

Signatures photographed on paper often come out grey or uneven. Use a dark pen on plain white paper, photograph it in good light, then use the signature preset — choosing Black & white makes the strokes crisp and the background clean. See how to make a clean signature image.

9. The website itself times out

On slow connections, large uploads can fail even below the limit. A smaller file uploads faster — another reason to stay comfortably under the maximum rather than right at it.

Quick checklist

  • Correct format (JPG for photo/signature, PDF for documents)
  • Within the KB range, in both 1000- and 1024-byte meanings
  • Exact pixel width × height if the form asks for it
  • Upright, plain background, face clearly visible
  • Always follow the official notice of your own form — requirements differ between forms and change over time.
